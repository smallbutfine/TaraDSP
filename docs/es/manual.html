1. What is TaraDSP?

TaraDSP is a professional audio processing tool that does one thing exceptionally well: FFT-based convolution. If you've ever used a guitar cabinet simulator, a convolution reverb, or a mastering chain with analog-modeled EQ, you've used convolution.

TaraDSP gives you that power in a lightweight, cross-platform package. Windows, macOS, Linux. CLI for batch processing, GUI for interactive work.

2. The Science of Convolution

Convolution applies the sonic characteristics of one signal (the impulse response) to another (your audio). When you play a dry guitar through a cabinet, the cabinet colors the sound — resonances, frequency response, decay. Convolution mathematically applies that coloring.

Why FFT? Direct convolution is O(n²) — billions of operations for a 1-second file. The FFT reduces this to O(n log n) via the convolution theorem:

Convolution in time = Multiplication in frequency
  1. FFT both signals → frequency domain
  2. Multiply them → one multiply per bin
  3. Inverse FFT → back to time domain

5000× speedup. PFFFT further accelerates with SIMD (SSE/NEON) — 4-8 samples per instruction.

5. Mastering Dither

The problem: Quantizing 24-bit to 16-bit without dither creates harmonic distortion at low levels.

The solution: TPDF — adds 2 LSB of triangular noise before quantization. Converts distortion to a constant noise floor — less audible, more natural.

TaraDSP's implementation:

  • TPDF noise: Two random values summed (triangular distribution)
  • Error feedback: Quantization error fed back, shaping the noise spectrum
  • Signal-dependent scaling: 70% dither at very low levels (< -90 dBFS)
When to use: Always dither for 16-bit. Never dither for 24/32-bit. Dither once, at the final output stage only.

6. Minimum Phase

Pre-ringing: Linear-phase IRs have symmetric energy — before AND after the main impulse. The energy before is pre-ringing, which sounds unnatural (in nature, sound arrives after, never before).

What minimum phase does: Transforms a linear-phase IR into minimum phase:

  • No pre-ringing
  • Lower latency
  • Same frequency response (magnitude unchanged)

Algorithm: Real cepstrum method — FFT → log magnitude → inverse FFT (cepstrum) → fold → FFT → complex spectrum → inverse FFT.

When to use: Always for cabinet simulation. Always for real-time. Optional for reverb (some prefer linear-phase spaciousness).

7. Resampling

Why: Mismatched rates (44.1k IR, 48k project), target device expects specific rate, CD release (44.1k).

Aliasing: Downsampling without filtering causes frequencies above Nyquist to fold back as metallic artifacts.

Engine priority:

  1. SoX Resampler (libsoxr) — VHQ, proper anti-aliasing. Best option.
  2. R8Brain — VHQ, used when libsoxr unavailable.
  3. FinalCD — external tool.
  4. Linear interpolation — fallback, no anti-aliasing.

Install libsoxr for best results. Ships as libsoxr.dll / .dylib / .so.0.

8. Fades and Trimming

Trim silence: IRs often have silence at start/end. Trim removes it:

  • -60 — typical for IRs
  • -80 — aggressive
  • 0 — disabled

Fades:

  • Fade-in: Always 1ms (automatic) — prevents start clicks
  • Fade-out: User-specified (-f <ms>) — prevents end clicks

Linear gain ramp: 1.0 → 0.0 (fade-out) or 0.0 → 1.0 (fade-in).

9. Practical Workflows

Guitar Cabinet Simulation

TaraDSP -x guitar_di.wav -y cab_57.wav -o guitar_amped.wav --min -l 2048 -b 24

Convolution Reverb

TaraDSP -x vocal_dry.wav -y concert_hall.wav -o vocal_reverb.wav -f 100 -b 24

Mastering with Dither

TaraDSP -x mix_24bit.wav -o master_16bit.wav -b 16

Sample Rate Conversion

TaraDSP -x recording_96k.wav -o master_44k1.wav -r 44100 -b 16

Batch Processing

for %f in (*.wav) do TaraDSP -x "%f" -y cab_ir.wav -o "processed_%f" --min -b 24

10. Combining Two IRs

TaraDSP can merge two impulse responses into a single new IR. This is useful for:

  • Cab + Mic blend: Combine a close-miked IR with a room-miked IR to create a custom blend
  • Cab + EQ: Convolve a cab IR with an EQ curve IR to bake the EQ into the cab
  • Two cabs blended: Mix two different speakers (e.g. 4x12 + 2x12) into one IR
  • IR + Reverb: Combine a cab IR with a short room IR for a "cabinet in a room" sound

How it works: Convolution is associative — convolving signal A with IR X, then with IR Y, is the same as convolving A with a single IR that is X convolved with Y. So to combine two IRs:

  1. Run TaraDSP with IR X as input and IR Y as the impulse response
  2. The output is a new WAV that is the combined IR
  3. Use that combined WAV as a normal IR in any convolution

Example: Blend two cabinet IRs

TaraDSP -x cab_close.wav -y cab_room.wav -o cab_blend.wav -b 32 --min

This convolves cab_close.wav with cab_room.wav, producing cab_blend.wav — a new IR that sounds like the close-miked cab played in the room. Use --min to keep it minimum phase, and -b 32 for float output (no quality loss).

Example: Bake EQ into a cab IR

TaraDSP -x cab_ir.wav -y eq_curve.wav -o cab_eq.wav -b 32 --min

Convolves the cab IR with an EQ curve (as an IR), baking the EQ into the cab. The result is a new cab IR with the EQ applied.

Example: Two-cab blend with level control

TaraDSP -x cab_a.wav -y cab_b.wav -o cab_ab.wav -b 32 --min -l 2048

Combines two cab IRs and truncates to 2048 samples for use in modelers. The relative loudness of the two cabs depends on their original levels — normalize the input IRs first if needed.

Tip: Always use -b 32 (32-bit float) for IR combination — it preserves the full dynamic range without quantization. Only reduce to 16/24-bit for the final output after all processing is done.
Tip: Use --min when combining IRs for cabinet simulation — it removes pre-ringing at each stage and keeps the result tight.

11. Tips from the Trenches

IR Selection

  • Shorter = tighter: 0.5s IR has less low-end rumble than 2s
  • Mic position matters: on-axis = bright, off-axis = dark
  • Distance adds room: close-miked = dry, room-miked = spacious
  • Minimum phase is almost always better for cabs

Level Management

  • Normalize after convolution, not before
  • Use 24-bit throughout — dither only at final output
  • Check mono compatibility — stereo IRs can have phase issues

Common Mistakes

  • Linear-phase IRs for cabs — always use --min
  • Dithering multiple times — dither once, at the end
  • Forgetting to normalize — convolution can boost 6-12 dB
  • 16-bit for intermediate files — use 24-bit or 32-bit
